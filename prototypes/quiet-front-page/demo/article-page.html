The public library that stays open after midnight

A small experiment in evening access has changed how an entire neighbourhood reads, studies and spends time together.

By Aoife Byrne

9 August 2026

The doors used to close just as commuters reached the square. Now the lights remain on until after midnight, and the old reading room has become one of the busiest public spaces in the district. Students arrive after work, parents bring children for a quiet hour, and older residents meet friends without having to buy anything.

The extended hours began as a modest six-week trial. Librarians expected a few dozen additional visitors each evening, but attendance quickly exceeded the daytime average. The building did not need to become louder or more commercial. It simply became available at the hours when many people could actually use it.

Local volunteers help with directions and basic administration, while trained staff remain responsible for collections and safeguarding. A neighbouring café provides flasks of tea, but no purchases are required. The arrangement has kept costs manageable without changing the character of the service.

Researchers studying the trial say its success is less about books alone than dependable civic space. People value somewhere warm, calm and well lit where they can stay without explaining themselves. That simple freedom is increasingly rare in the centre of a city.

The council has now approved the later opening hours for another year. Other branches are considering similar trials, adapted to local transport and staffing. For regular visitors, however, the most important change is already ordinary: the library is there when they need it.
